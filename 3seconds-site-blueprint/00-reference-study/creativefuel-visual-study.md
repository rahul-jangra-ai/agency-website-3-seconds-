# Creativefuel.io — Visual & Experience Study

> Research scope: visual appearance, public page structure, imagery, content rhythm, and interaction ideas. No source-code implementation is copied into this blueprint.

## What stands out

### 1. Oversized editorial statements
The reference site uses short, bold headlines as major visual objects rather than treating headings as ordinary labels. The home page opens with a large statement around creating memorable moments, and the About page similarly uses a stacked, oversized opening line.

**3seconds takeaway:** make language itself part of the art direction. Headlines can occupy 50–80% of the viewport and should break into intentional lines.

### 2. Playful illustration + culture-led imagery
The home hero publicly exposes a collage/illustration of recognizable internet-culture figures using a limited black/gray/yellow palette. Project work is then much more colorful, allowing campaign visuals to take over.

**3seconds takeaway:** keep the 3seconds shell controlled and graphic; let work imagery provide the color explosions.

### 3. Long-scroll narrative, not a corporate brochure
The home page does not stop at a simple hero/services/contact pattern. It moves through About, large facts/statistics, a culture/timeline idea, case studies, owned/community reach, expertise, clients, and a footer CTA.

**3seconds takeaway:** build an intentional story arc. Each scroll should reveal a new reason to trust the agency.

### 4. Work is image-first
The Projects/Works page uses a strong introductory statement, category filters, and a large visual project catalogue. The portfolio is not treated as a small supporting section; it is a core product.

**3seconds takeaway:** `/work` should be one of the strongest routes, with large thumbnails, video loops, filtering, and very little explanatory UI.

### 5. Services are extensive but repeated consistently
The Services page contains multiple service categories with repeated explanatory patterns: what it is, why the agency, and supporting points.

**3seconds takeaway:** use a consistent expandable service system rather than 12 separate visual treatments. Let motion and media vary while information architecture stays stable.

### 6. Proof appears repeatedly
The reference experience reinforces scale through campaign counts, large audience/follower metrics, case studies, creator/community reach, and client logos.

**3seconds takeaway:** proof should appear in several formats: quantified outcomes, recognizable work, testimonial quotes, and client marks.

### 7. Contact is direct
The Contact route exposes direct channels plus a project form. The message is straightforward: reach the team or submit a brief.

**3seconds takeaway:** make contact feel like starting a creative brief, not filling an administrative form.

## Apparent motion/scroll language to borrow as principles
Exact animation timings are not treated as reference requirements. The public experience supports a motion-heavy, scroll-led reading of the site. For 3seconds, translate that into:
- line-masked headline reveals;
- staggered media entrances;
- sticky scroll chapters;
- horizontal project movement controlled by vertical scroll;
- looping logo/client marquees;
- counters that animate once in-view;
- subtle parallax on oversized media;
- filter transitions on portfolio items;
- page transitions that preserve momentum.

## What NOT to copy
- exact yellow/gray hero illustration style;
- exact section order;
- any Creativefuel copy, projects, clients, stats, or service language;
- exact menu jokes or footer wording;
- exact page composition or proprietary media.
